# DWA 490: 80/20 Reading Assignment Proposal
### Senior Comprehensives | Spring 2026

---

## Executive Summary

This proposal applies the **Pareto principle (80/20 rule)** to assign methodological readings for DWA 490. The goal is to identify the **~20% of readings** that will deliver **~80% of the methodological value** for students writing their senior theses.

The selected readings focus on research design fundamentals, case selection, variation, and literature review writing—the core skills students need during the intensive thesis-writing semester.

---

## Guiding Principles for Selection

- **Actionability:** Readings that students can immediately apply to their thesis work
- **Accessibility:** Prioritize shorter, more focused pieces over comprehensive textbooks  
- **Timing:** Match readings to the phase of thesis work (outline → draft → revision)
- **Complementarity:** Readings should reinforce each other without excessive overlap

## Recommended Reading Assignments by Week

### Week 2 (Jan 27): "Variation, Variation, Variation" — Research Design Fundamentals

| Reading | Focus / Key Sections |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Gerring (2004)** *"What Is a Case Study and What Is It Good For?"* | **ENTIRE ARTICLE (~25 pp.)** Core concepts: What makes a case study? Research design typology (Table 1). When case studies are most appropriate. Exploratory vs. confirmatory research. |
| **Seawright & Gerring (2008)** *"Case Selection Techniques in Case Study Research"* | **pp. 294-300 ONLY (~7 pp.)** Introduction through "Typical Cases." Why purposive case selection matters. Overview of the seven case types (Table 1). *Save detailed discussion of each type for Week 3.* |
| **Gerring (2012)** *"Mere Description"* | **pp. 721-733 (~12 pp.)** What is description? Why description matters for causal inference. Types of descriptive inference. Helps students doing descriptive theses understand their contribution. |
| **Collier (2011)** *"Understanding Process Tracing"* | **Table 1 + pp. 823-830 (~8 pp.)** The four tests for causal inference (hoop, smoking gun, doubly decisive, straw-in-the-wind). Concrete examples of within-case analysis. |
| *OPTIONAL:* **Van Evera** *Guide to Methods* | *Ch. 2: "Hypotheses, Laws, and Theories" (pp. 7-48).* The original formulation of the four tests. More detailed than Collier but covers same conceptual ground. |

> **Week 2 Total: ~52 pages required** | *These readings establish the conceptual vocabulary for research design and introduce case selection logic.*

| Reading | Focus / Key Sections |

### Week 5 (Feb 17): Literature Review

| Reading | Focus / Key Sections |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Cantero (SJSU)** *"How to Write a Literature Review"* | **ENTIRE GUIDE (~7 pp.)** Concise, practical guide. What a lit review is (and isn't). Three organizational types: chronological, thematic, methodological. Summary vs. synthesis vs. evaluation. Introduction-body-conclusion structure. |
| **Booth et al.** *The Craft of Research* | **Chapter 6: "Engaging Sources" (~15 pp.)** How to read sources strategically. Taking notes that will be useful for writing. Managing sources and avoiding getting lost in material. |
| **King** *"Publication, Publication"* | **Section 8: "Literature Review" ONLY (~1 p.)** Key insight: Do NOT include a standalone "Literature Review" section—weave sources into your argument. Brief but valuable professional advice. |

> **Week 5 Total: ~25 pages** | *Students should submit a literature review draft with this week's assignment.*

## Key Takeaways for Students

1. **Know your case selection logic:** Be able to explain why your case(s) are typical, deviant, most similar, etc.

2. **Understand what tests your evidence provides:** Hoop test? Smoking gun? Know the difference.

3. **Synthesize, don't summarize:** Your lit review should show how sources relate to each other and to your argument.

4. **Focus on variation:** What varies in your study? Your IV? Your DV? Across cases? Over time?

## Quick Reference: The Seven Case Selection Types (Seawright & Gerring)

| Type | Definition | Best Use |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Typical** | Representative of cross-case relationship | Confirmatory; probe causal mechanisms |
| **Diverse** | Exemplify diverse values of X, Y, or X/Y | Exploratory or confirmatory; full range of variation |
| **Extreme** | Extreme/unusual values of X or Y | Exploratory; open-ended probe |
| **Deviant** | Surprising value relative to theory | Exploratory; probe new explanations |
| **Influential** | Influential configurations of IVs | Confirmatory; double-check influential cases |
| **Most Similar** | Similar background, different X₁ and Y | Confirmatory; attribute Y to X₁ |
| **Most Different** | Different background, same X₁ and Y | Confirmatory (weaker than most similar) |

---

## Quick Reference: The Four Tests (Van Evera / Collier)

| Test | Passing | Failing | Analogy |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Straw-in-the-Wind** | Affirms relevance, doesn't confirm | Slightly weakens | Circumstantial evidence |
| **Hoop** | Affirms relevance, doesn't confirm | **Eliminates hypothesis** | "Was suspect in state?" |
| **Smoking Gun** | **Confirms hypothesis** | Doesn't eliminate | Gun in suspect's hand |
| **Doubly Decisive** | **Confirms AND eliminates others** | **Eliminates hypothesis** | Bank camera footage |
