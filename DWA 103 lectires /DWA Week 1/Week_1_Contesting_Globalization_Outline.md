# Week 1: Contesting Globalization
## DWA 103 — The Contested World Economy

---

### Presentation Overview

**Course Text:** Eric Helleiner, *The Contested World Economy: The Deep and Global Roots of International Political Economy* (Cambridge University Press, 2023)

**Key Argument:** The field of International Political Economy (IPE) has deeper and more globally diverse intellectual roots than conventional textbooks suggest. Pre-1945 debates about the world economy were not limited to European and American thinkers, nor confined to the trilateral debate between economic liberals, neomercantilists, and Marxists.

---

## I. What is International Political Economy?

### Definition & Origins
IPE examines the intersection of politics and economics at the international level—how states, markets, and societies interact in the global economy.

The formal field of IPE emerged in the early 1970s, catalyzed by:
- The end of dollar-gold convertibility (1971)
- The OPEC oil crisis (1973)
- Calls for a New International Economic Order (1974)

### The "Rebirth" Thesis
> Helleiner argues the field was "more a rebirth or revival than a birth" (citing Susan Strange)

Early IPE scholars drew heavily on pre-1945 political economists, yet this deep history remains understudied.

---

## II. The Book's Central Claims

### Claim 1: A More Global History
Standard IPE textbooks feature thinkers primarily from Europe and the United States. Helleiner demonstrates that:
- Thinkers from Asia, Africa, Latin America, and the Middle East made significant contributions
- Non-European thinkers adapted European ideas in innovative ways
- Some developed independent versions of economic thought
- Ideas flowed in multiple directions—not just from West to rest

### Claim 2: Beyond the "Three Orthodoxies"
Conventional IPE history focuses on three perspectives:
1. **Economic Liberalism**
2. **Neomercantilism**
3. **Marxism**

Helleiner shows pre-1945 debates were far richer, including:
- Autarkism (national self-sufficiency)
- Environmentalism
- Feminism
- Pan-Africanism
- Pan-Islamism
- Pan-Asianism
- Distinctive visions of economic regionalism

---

## III. The Three Orthodoxies — Overview

### A. Economic Liberalism

**Core Beliefs:**
- Free trade and free markets foster global prosperity
- International commerce promotes peace between nations
- Individual liberty is enhanced by economic freedom

**Key Figures (European):**
- Adam Smith (1723–1790)
- David Ricardo (1772–1823)
- Richard Cobden (1804–1865)
- John Stuart Mill (1806–1873)
- Walter Bagehot (1826–1877)

**Smith's Famous Ideas:**
> "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own self-interest."

Smith critiqued the "mercantile system" of government-directed trade, advocating instead for a "simple system of natural liberty."

**Internal Divisions:**
Economic liberals disagreed on:
- Universal applicability of liberal principles
- Extent of acceptable specialization
- Exceptions to free trade (e.g., national defense)
- Role of imperialism and colonialism
- Need for intergovernmental cooperation

**Non-European Adaptations:**
Thinkers worldwide adapted liberalism to local contexts:
- **Americas:** José da Silva Lisboa (Brazil), Carlos Calvo (Argentina)
- **South Asia:** Rammohun Roy, Dadabhai Naoroji
- **East Asia:** Taguchi Ukichi (Japan), Yan Fu (China)
- **Africa/Ottoman Empire:** Olaudah Equiano, Hassuna D'Ghies

Some claimed independent origins for liberal ideas—Chinese scholars argued Confucian texts had endorsed market-oriented policies centuries before Smith.

---

### B. Neomercantilism

**Core Beliefs:**
- Reject liberal free trade advocacy
- Strategic protectionism builds state wealth and power
- Government activism is necessary to develop industry
- National economic development requires state intervention

**Key Figures:**
- Alexander Hamilton (1755–1804)
- Friedrich List (1789–1846)
- Henry Carey (1793–1879)
- Gustav Schmoller (1838–1917)
- Sergei Witte (1849–1915)

**Hamilton's 1791 Report on Manufactures:**
Argued the U.S. government must actively promote manufacturing for:
- National "independence and security"
- Increased productivity and employment
- Reduced dependence on unreliable foreign markets

**List's "Infant Industry" Argument:**
> "It is a very common device that when anyone has attained the summit of greatness, he kicks away the ladder by which he has climbed up, in order to deprive others of the means of climbing up after him."

List criticized Britain for advocating free trade after building its own industrial power through protectionism.

**Key Concept: Productive Power**
> List argued "the power of producing wealth is... infinitely more important than wealth itself."

He believed industrialization boosted national "productive power"—the foundation of long-term wealth and strength.

**Internal Divisions:**
Neomercantilists disagreed on:
- Purposes and duration of protectionism
- Views on imperialism and colonialism
- Universal applicability of their policies
- Long-term goals (some saw protectionism as temporary path to eventual free trade)

**Non-European Neomercantilism:**
- **Japan:** Indigenous neomercantilist thought predated Western contact
- **China:** Sun Yat-sen's developmental nationalism
- **India:** Mahadev Govind Ranade's economic nationalism
- **Latin America:** Various developmental thinkers

---

### C. Marxism

**Core Beliefs:**
- Capitalism is characterized by class conflict and exploitation
- The bourgeoisie extracts surplus value from the proletariat
- Free trade extends exploitation globally
- Revolutionary overthrow of capitalism is necessary

**Key Figures:**
- Karl Marx (1818–1883)
- Friedrich Engels (1820–1895)
- Vladimir Lenin (1870–1924)
- Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919)

**Marx on Global Capitalism:**
> "The bourgeoisie, by the rapid improvement of all instruments of production, by the immensely facilitated means of communication, draws all nations, even the most barbarian, into civilization."

Marx saw capitalism as inherently globalizing—creating "a world after its own image."

**Critique of Economic Liberalism:**
> Marx called the liberal notion of free trade fostering "brotherhood" among nations an idea "that could only be engendered in the brain of the bourgeoisie."

He saw cosmopolitan exploitation, not harmony.

**Critique of Neomercantilism:**
Marx criticized List's focus on British domination as missing the point—the real oppression was class-based, not national. Protectionism would simply allow the German bourgeoisie to exploit their fellow countrymen.

**Theories of Imperialism:**
Lenin and Luxemburg developed influential Marxist explanations for late 19th/early 20th century imperialism:
- **Lenin:** Imperialism as the "highest stage" of capitalism driven by finance capital seeking foreign investment
- **Luxemburg:** Capitalist accumulation requires continuous expansion into non-capitalist territories

**Global Diffusion:**
Marxism spread worldwide but was adapted locally:
- **Japan:** Kōtoku Shūsui, Takahashi Kamekichi
- **India:** M.N. Roy
- **China:** Adaptations leading to Maoism
- **Latin America:** José Carlos Mariátegui (Peru)
- **Caribbean:** George Padmore, C.L.R. James

## IV. Beyond the Three Orthodoxies — Preview

Helleiner's book devotes nearly half its content to perspectives beyond the traditional three, including:

### Autarkism
Advocates of national economic self-sufficiency who went beyond neomercantilist protectionism to call for reduced dependence on foreign trade altogether.

### Environmentalism
Pre-1945 thinkers who critiqued industrial capitalism's environmental degradation—anticipating modern sustainability concerns.

### Feminism
Thinkers who highlighted the patriarchal nature of the world economy and women's subordinate economic position.

### Pan-Africanism
> Helleiner: Pan-Africanists "sought to cultivate the economic solidarity of Africans and the African diaspora in order to challenge their subordinate position in the world economy."

Key figures: W.E.B. Du Bois, Marcus Garvey, Hubert Harrison

### Pan-Islamism & Pan-Asianism
Religious and civilizational perspectives that invoked transnational identities to resist Western economic domination.

### Economic Regionalism
Distinctive visions for regional economic integration in East Asia, Europe, and the Americas.

---

## V. Why This History Matters

### For Understanding Contemporary Debates
Many current IPE topics—environmental degradation, gender inequality, racial discrimination, religious worldviews, varieties of regionalism—have deep historical roots that standard textbooks overlook.

### For Building "Global Conversations"
> Helleiner aims to "build more 'global conversations' among IPE scholars across the globe."

Understanding diverse intellectual traditions can enrich contemporary analysis.

### Against Intellectual Path Dependence
The narrow focus on three European/American perspectives in textbooks may reflect:
- Concerns of those who pioneered formal IPE in the 1970s
- The agenda-setting power of Bretton Woods as a "foundational moment"
- Simple intellectual inertia

| **IPE** | International Political Economy—the study of politics and economics at the global level |
| **Economic Liberalism** | Perspective advocating free trade and free markets |

| **Marxism** | Perspective emphasizing class conflict and capitalist exploitation |
| **Autarky** | National economic self-sufficiency |
| **Infant Industry Protection** | Temporary protectionism to allow new industries to develop |
| **Productive Power** | List's concept of a nation's broad economic capacity |
