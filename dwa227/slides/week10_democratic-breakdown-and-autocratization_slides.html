2 · Measurement · the debate, summarized

Is the world backsliding?

Little & Meng 2024
  • “Objective” indicators show no global decline in the past decade
  • Turnover steady since late 1990s; winners’ vote and seat shares down; multiparty competition and executive limits steady; no rise in term-limit evasion
  • Gap with expert indices: coder bias (more media coverage) or subtler tactics
  • Global decline invisible to objective measures = “an extraordinary claim”; onus on the alarmists
Miller 2024 · Knutsen et al. 2024 (V-Dem)
  • Decline is inside democracies; objective indicators sort democracy from autocracy but miss quality within democracy (“streetlight”)
  • Within democracies, V-Dem, Freedom House, and Polity all fall from the late 2000s: ≈ 1 in 9 democracies’ worth
  • No evidence of “bad-vibes bias” among experts
  • “Objective” coding also hides judgments; among democracies the two indices correlate at 0.16
  • Common ground: backsliding in Hungary and Venezuela (Little & Meng 2024: 150); “They do not dispute the coding of any cases” (Miller 2024: 203)

PS: Political Science & Politics 57(2), April 2024: Little & Meng 149–161; Miller 202–203; Knutsen et al. 162.

3 · Episodes · democracies entering autocratization, 1900–2019

Outcomes of 96 episodes

Universe. 293 autocratization episodes, 1900–2019; 96 began in a democracy.

Episode. An annual drop of at least 0.01 on V-Dem’s Electoral Democracy Index, with an overall decline of at least 0.10.

Breakdown. The regime is reclassified as an autocracy before the episode ends. Averted. It stays a democracy to the end of the episode.

Undetermined. The episode is still running; the outcome is not yet known.

Appendix · 2 · Measurement · Knutsen et al. 2024 (V-Dem)

Correlation of two indices, all countries, 1980–2020

EDI — V-Dem’s Electoral Democracy Index (v2x_polyarchy). V-Dem’s measure of Robert Dahl’s “polyarchy”: how far a country achieves electoral democracy. It combines five components — freedom of association, clean elections, freedom of expression and alternative sources of information, elected officials, and suffrage — built from country experts’ ratings. Scale 0–1 (V-Dem v16, 2025: Denmark 0.91, U.S. 0.74, Hungary 0.42, Russia 0.17). Knutsen et al. split the sample at EDI 0.4.

Little & Meng’s “objective” index. The average of variables rescaled to 0–1: suffrage; presidential vote shares and winning-party seat shares; incumbent party’s time in office; legislative and executive competitiveness; whether the incumbent party lost the last election; NELDA multiparty and process-violation indices; term limits, succession and dismissal rules (Little & Meng 2024: 156).

ρ (correlation). 1 = the two indices rank country-years identically; 0 = no relationship. Below EDI 0.4 the two agree moderately (0.56); at 0.4 and above they barely agree (0.16). Knutsen et al.: “Clearly, the two indices measure democracy very differently.”

Also in the reply. Tests for a “bad-vibes bias” among V-Dem experts: “We find no evidence for these claims.” And: “coding seemingly objective indicators typically requires multiple hidden judgments by human raters.”

Knutsen, Marquardt, Seim, Coppedge et al., PS 57(2), 2024: 162 (figure 1). EDI definition: V-Dem Codebook v16.
