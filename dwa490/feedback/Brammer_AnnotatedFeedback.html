Introduction: Russo-Ukrainian War (pp. 1–2)

Since the escalation of the Russo-Ukrainian war in February 2022, Ukraine has faced one of the largest displacement crises in Europe since the Second World War. Millions of Ukrainians have been forced to flee their homes, with many becoming internally displaced persons (IDPs) seeking safety in western regions of the country. Cities near the western border, such as Uzhhorod near the Slovakian border, have transformed into critical hubs for displaced populations, humanitarian logistics and civilian support networks. These regions, once peripheral to Ukraine's political and economic center, now play an essential role in sustaining the country's social and institutional resilience.

While such scholarly and media attention has focused on military developments and geopolitical implications, far less attention has been given to the everyday experiences of civilians adapting to wartime conditions. In particular, the role of non-militant service providers (individuals who continue to operate clinics, businesses and community support structures) remains underexplored in existing research on conflict and resilience. These actors often function as informal pillars of social stability, helping displaced populations rebuild livelihoods and maintain a sense of normalcy despite ongoing violence.

This research examines how wartime conditions shape the resilient and supportive strategies of Ukrainian civilians, particularly among internally displaced populations and the community members who support them. Using a comparative case study approach, this research focuses on two Ukrainian civilians in Uzhhorod whose work has become integral for their local community. This research question guiding this case study is: How do wartime conditions shape the resilient and supportive strategies of Ukrainian civilians and how do these strategies undermine continued Russian aggression?

This research argues that wartime conditions have intensified, rather than weakened, civilian resilience and mutual support networks in Ukraine. Through the story of two brothers whose professional roles have evolved during the war, this research demonstrates how individuals and communities adapt to uncertainty and reduced international support by developing new forms of cooperation and solidarity.

The cases examined illustrate how resilience is not simply the ability to endure hardship, but the capacity to transform adversity into collective action. In doing so, Ukrainian civilians continue to undermine the strategic objectives of Russian aggression by sustaining social cohesion, rebuilding livelihoods and therefore reinforcing national identity.

Literature Review: Resilience and Civil Society in Conflict (pp. 5–7)

The concept of resilience has become increasingly central in scholarship on conflict and humanitarian crises. Social resilience refers to the capacity of individuals and communities to adapt to disruption while maintaining essential functions and social structures. Rather than viewing populations solely as victims of conflict, resilience theory emphasizes the agency of communities in responding to adversity.

In the context of armed conflict, resilience often manifests through informal support networks, local institutions, and adaptive strategies that enable communities to cope with displacement, economic disruption, and insecurity. These forms of resilience frequently emerge from civil society rather than formal state structures. As a result, service providers such as medical practitioners play a crucial role in sustaining community stability during wartime.

Research on civilian resistance also highlights the importance of non-violent forms of defiance and support demonstrates that civilian participation and non-violent resistance movements can significantly shape the trajectory of conflicts. In Ukraine, such participation often occurs through volunteer networks, humanitarian initiatives and local service provision rather than direct political mobilization.

Migration and IDPs: The war has produced millions of IDPs within Ukraine. Studies of forced migration emphasize that displacement experiences vary significantly depending on socioeconomic status, access to networks and local infrastructure. IDPs frequently rely on host communities and local service providers to navigate housing, employment, healthcare, and social integration.
