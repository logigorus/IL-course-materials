Syllabus, Week 22

Classical Economic Liberalism: Core Beliefs

  • First perspective on political economy to reach a worldwide audience
  • Smith's Wealth of Nations (1776) achieved near-universal recognition by the early 20th century

1

Free trade generates global prosperity

2

Free markets promote international peace

3

Economic freedom secures individual freedom

Beyond these three, disagreements existed among liberals.

Helleiner 2023, ch. 23

Smith: How Markets Generate Prosperity

  • Natural propensity "to truck, barter, and exchange"
  • Division of labor → productivity
  • Price mechanism → decentralized coordination
  • Self-interest → collective benefit

Three legitimate duties of government

  • Protection from foreign invasion
  • Administration of justice
  • Public works "which it can never be for the interest of any individual" to provide
Smith 1776 · Helleiner 2023, ch. 24

The "Invisible Hand"

  • Used once in The Wealth of Nations
  • Rulers "exposed to innumerable delusions"; no "human wisdom or knowledge" sufficient for central planning
"It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own self-interest." — Adam Smith, The Wealth of Nations, 1776
Smith 1776 · Helleiner 2023: 205

Smith's Case for Free Trade

Efficient allocationRestrictions give protected firms "monopoly of the home market," drawing capital from more valuable uses
Consumer benefitFree trade prevents consumers being "sacrificed" to inefficient domestic monopolies
Outlet for surplusTrade employs resources that would otherwise sit idle
Dynamic gainsCompetitive pressure, larger markets, "mutual communication of knowledge, and of all sorts of improvements" (Smith 1776)
Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, pp. 20–236

Free Trade and Individual Freedom

  • Protectionism = an "oppressive policy" against natural liberty to trade (Smith 1776)
  • Rooted in natural law: "the right to engage in commerce pertains equally to all peoples" (Grotius 1604, in Helleiner 2023: 21)
  • Anti-rent-seeking: protection defends elite monopoly privileges over the interests of the many
  • Applied globally: colonial trade restrictions on America a "manifest violation of the most sacred rights of mankind," published months before the Declaration of Independence (Smith 1776; Helleiner 2023: 22)
Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, pp. 21–237

Smith's Exceptions to Free Trade

RetaliationJustified only if likely to get a trading partner's restrictions lifted
Gradual transitionSudden liberalization after long protection could cause mass job loss; "freedom of trade should be restored only by slow gradations" (Smith 1776)
Tax equalizationTariffs to offset foreign goods taxed lower abroad
National defenseEndorsed England's Navigation Acts: "the wisest of all the commercial regulations of England" (Smith 1776)

Smith evaluated policy by state wealth and power — more cautious than many 19th-century followers.

Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, pp. 24–268

Ricardo: Comparative Advantage

  • Complete specialization, even if one country is absolutely more efficient at both goods
  • Gains extend to "the universal society of nations"

Watson 2017: Ricardo's abstraction omits the coercive 1703 treaty and Brazilian-gold financing behind the actual England–Portugal trade.

Ricardo 1817 · Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, p. 279

Mill and Cobden: Trade as Peace

  • Mill: free trade "rapidly rendering war obsolete, by strengthening and multiplying the personal interests which are in natural opposition to it" (Mill [1871]1965: 581)
  • Mill's exceptions: national defense ("invidious exception"); temporary protection for a "young and rising nation" (Mill, in Helleiner 2023: 29)
  • Cobden: repeal of the Corn Laws (1846); 1860 Cobden–Chevalier treaty with an unconditional most-favored-nation clause
  • Cobden called free trade a "divine law" for the world
Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, pp. 28–3210
Contested Case

Free Trade by Force: The Opium Wars

  • Britain forced open Chinese markets, 1839–42 and 1856–60
  • Many British liberals supported the action against a government seen as blocking its own people's benefit from trade
  • Cobden opposed the Second Opium War, rejecting the "barbarian" label for China and calling Britain's force the "barbaric act"
"In many cases, the eagerness of England in the Free-trade cause only does that cause harm." — Walter Bagehot, on the 100th anniversary of The Wealth of Nations, in Helleiner 2023: 31
Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, pp. 31–3211

Bagehot: Is Liberalism Universal?

  • Economic liberalism a theory "proved of, and applicable to, particular societies only" — those with "grown-up competitive commerce" (Bagehot [1876]1885: 6, 31, 27, 9)
  • Called it "English political economy" — contrast with Smith's claim to universal relevance
  • A more modest claim than Cobden's "divine law"
Helleiner 2023, ch. 2, p. 3112
Contested Case · Domestic Backlash

Bryan and the Gold Standard, 1896

  • Democratic National Convention, July 9, 1896: William Jennings Bryan argues against the gold standard, for free coinage of silver
  • Debtor farmers of the South and West vs. Eastern creditor and financial interests — a domestic fight over a pillar of liberal international monetary order
  • Video (1921 recording, audio restored): youtube.com/watch?v=UV2wRCcWJa8
"You shall not press down upon the brow of labor this crown of thorns; you shall not crucify mankind upon a cross of gold." — William Jennings Bryan, Democratic National Convention, July 9, 1896

Case

Equiano, The Interesting Narrative, 1789

  • One of the first published narratives of an enslaved person's experience of the Middle Passage
  • Equiano used free-trade language against slavery: "legitimate commerce" with Africa as a substitute for the slave trade (Equiano [1789]2001)
  • Performance: actor Jyuddah Jaymes reads extracts from The Interesting Narrative — youtube.com/watch?v=EPkIouAElGA
Equiano [1789]2001 · Helleiner 2023, ch. 3, p. 4713b
Case

Naoroji's "Drain Theory," 1867

  • First detailed economic critique of colonialism by a colonized subject
  • British rule drained Indian wealth via remittances and debt-service payments to Britain
  • Engaged Mill's own liberal framework to argue India needed infant-industry protection: "free trade between England and India in a matter like this is something like a race between a starving, exhausting invalid and a strong man with a horse to ride on" (Naoroji, in Helleiner 2023: 45)
Helleiner 2023, ch. 3, pp. 44–4614

Liberal Imperialism: A Debate Inside Liberalism

Mill

Colonial "despotism" justified paternalistically — colonized peoples "not yet ready" for self-government; worked for the East India Company, 1823–58

Roy · Naoroji

Engaged liberal imperialism sympathetically at first, hoping British rule could be a progressive force; Naoroji later called for self-rule

Cobden

Opposed the use of force to spread free trade; rejected the "barbarian" framing of China

Helleiner 2023, ch. 3, pp. 42–4615

20th-Century Liberal IPE: Three Strands

  • Central question: how can states cooperate under anarchy?
Commercial liberalismTrade creates interdependence; war becomes costly
Institutional liberalismRules, norms, and organizations facilitate cooperation
Republican liberalismDomestic regime type shapes foreign policy; democracies don't fight each other
Aggarwal & Dupont16

Why Is Cooperation Difficult?

Prisoner's DilemmaMutual cooperation best, but defection is the dominant strategy
Stag HuntCoordination game; cooperation preferred only if assured of the other side

Two stumbling blocks

  • Uncertainty — can I trust others to cooperate?
  • Free riding — why pay costs if I can benefit from others' efforts?

How Institutions Solve Cooperation Problems

Reduce info costsFora for communication; open procedures
Enable monitoringTrack compliance; identify defectors

Lake: Open Economy Politics Depth

  • Trade policy reflects domestic political coalitions, not just an undifferentiated "national interest"
InternationalStructure of the global economy; rules of trade
DomesticDistribution of interests and preferences among groups
SectoralWho wins and loses from a given trade policy?

Applied to Helleiner: explains why Britain moved to free trade in the 1840s (industrial interests) while others resisted (agricultural interests, infant industries).
