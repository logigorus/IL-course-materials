Key themes
  • Gains from exchange; comparative advantage (Smith, Ricardo)
  • Free trade as the road to prosperity and peace (Cobden; Britain repeals the Corn Laws, 1846)

  • Relative gains: “Who will gain more?” (Waltz; Kirshner)
  • Economic nationalism: infant industries, the productive powers of the nation (Hamilton, List; Bismarck’s tariff, 1879)
  • Interdependence as vulnerability (Hirschman); weaponized interdependence (Farrell & Newman)
  • Three realist traditions: building power, using leverage, paying for security

Pivot · two kinds of fact

Rationalist tools, in Abdelal’s description

“materialist — in the sense that the incentive structures faced by agents are composed primarily of the material facts of the world, those facts that exist irrespective of our interpretation of them.”Abdelal 2009: 63

Constructivist tools

“constructivist approaches assume that the economy consists of both material facts and social facts.” … “these facts by themselves are indeterminate”Abdelal 2009: 73

Logic 4 · Subjectivity · central banks and the net-zero alliances, 2017–25

NGFS · Network for Greening the Financial System
  • Central banks and supervisors, founded Dec 2017 at the Paris One Planet Summit by eight members; “over 171 Members and Observers” now
  • Purpose: “to enhance the role of the financial system to manage risks and to mobilise mainstream finance”
  • The Federal Reserve joined Dec 15 2020; withdrew Jan 17 2025
NZBA · Net-Zero Banking Alliance
  • Commercial banks, convened by UNEP FI; launched Apr 21 2021 with 43 banks; a founding member of GFANZ (Carney, COP26)
  • Commitment: align “lending and investment portfolios … with pathways to net-zero by 2050 or sooner”
the work of the NGFS has increasingly broadened in scope, covering a wider range of issues that are outside of the Board’s statutory mandate. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, press release, Jan 17 2025
