II · Does capitalism need democracy? · Friedman

History suggests only that capitalism is a necessary condition for political freedom. Clearly it is not a sufficient condition. Milton Friedman, Capitalism and Freedom (1962), ch. 1, “The Relation between Economic Freedom and Political Freedom” (excerpt p. 2)

II · Does capitalism need democracy? · Friedman · the market as a check

Two ways of co-ordinating “the economic activities of millions” (p. 4)

“central direction involving the use of coercion — the technique of the army and of the modern totalitarian state”

“voluntary co-operation of individuals — the technique of the market place”

“if economic power is joined to political power, concentration seems almost inevitable. On the other hand, if economic power is kept in separate hands from political power, it can serve as a check and a counter to political power.”

III · Can dictatorships deliver growth? · Rodrik

When we look at systematic historical evidence, instead of individual cases, we find that authoritarianism buys little in terms of economic growth. For every authoritarian country that has managed to grow rapidly, there are several that have floundered. For every Lee Kuan Yew of Singapore, there are many like Mobutu Sese Seko of the Congo. Dani Rodrik, “The Myth of Authoritarian Growth,” Project Syndicate, August 2010

IV · Holding on · Sharafutdinova & Dawisha · Russia’s billionaires

“The best of both worlds”

As Olson argued, stationary bandits have a stake in seeing their sovereign dominion grow and prosper and therefore they tax less, commit more credibly, and provide other public goods… p. 362
These business elites that take advantage of weak institutions at home for profit maximization rely on the best of both worlds—secure institutions in the West to protect profits over the long term and weak institutions in Russia to get super-profits in the short term. p. 369

Gulnaz Sharafutdinova & Karen Dawisha, Perspectives on Politics 15(2), 2017 (not assigned in this course). Cf. Week 7, Global Finance.

IV · Holding on · Applebaum · Autocracy, Inc.

Nowadays, autocracies are run not by one bad guy but by sophisticated networks relying on kleptocratic financial structures, a complex of security services—military, paramilitary, police—and technological experts who provide surveillance, propaganda, and disinformation.
  • “not like a bloc but rather like an agglomeration of companies, bound not by ideology but rather by a ruthless, single-minded determination to preserve their personal wealth and power”

Anne Applebaum, Autocracy, Inc. (2024), Introduction, as posted by the author, June 16, 2024; unpaginated (not assigned in this course).
