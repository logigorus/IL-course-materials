The pivot · unit of analysis

When faced with the possibility of cooperating for mutual gain, states that feel insecure must ask how the gain will be divided. They are compelled to ask not ‘Will both of us gain?’ but ‘Who will gain more?’ Kenneth Waltz, Theory of International Politics (1979: 105), quoted in Drezner 2010: 11

Also: List, “the interest of individuals and the interest of the commerce of a whole nation are widely different things” (List 1885: 269, quoted in Kirshner 2009: 39).

Realism · three foundations

Realist political economy rests upon three foundations: the state, pursuing the national interest, in an environment defined by anarchy. Kirshner 2009: 36
State
“neither the sum of individual interests à la liberalism nor the implicit or explicit representative of certain privileged interests within society”
National interest
“distinct from a pluralist vision that derives the national interest from the summation of individual interests”
Anarchy
“the lack of an ultimate international authority, and thus the absence of any guarantee that the nation will not be invaded, overrun, conquered, and pillaged”

Kirshner 2009: 36. Same page: “Security is a public good; public goods tend to be underprovided by private actors; the state is responsible for the provision of security.”

Realism · relative gains

In a phrase, “realists aren’t in it for the money.” That is, actors behaving as realists would expect them to … will often, without regret, trade wealth for power (or, when secure, status). Kirshner 2009: 37
[T]he wealth and might of a nation depends “principally on whether its neighbors possess more or less of it. For power and riches have become a relative matter.” P. W. von Hornigk, 1684, quoted in Kirshner 2009: 39

Also Kirshner 2009: 40, quoting Grieco (1990: 217): “states in anarchy must fear that others may seek to destroy or enslave them.” Viner’s four propositions on wealth and power (1948: 10) in Drezner 2010: 4.

A note on labels · realism, economic nationalism, neomercantilism

The essence of the mercantilist perspective, whether it is labeled economic nationalism, protectionism, or the doctrine of the German Historical School, is the subservience of the economy to the state and its interests – interests that range from matters of domestic welfare to those of international security. Robert Gilpin (1975: 25), quoted in Drezner 2010: 8
The “neomercantilist” perspective is sometimes referred to as an “economic nationalist” or “realist” one in IPE textbooks. I have discussed elsewhere why I think “neomercantilism” is the more useful label. CWE p. 6, n. 14

Schmoller “embraced the label ‘neomercantilism’ as a description for the policy program he backed (in contrast to List, who did not use this term)” (CWE p. 63). Drezner 2010: 9: “While realists and mercantilists might agree on the role that power plays in the global economy, they do not necessarily agree on the normative implications of that insight.”

1 · Building power · Hamilton 1791, List 1841

Not only the wealth but the independence and security of a country appear to be materially connected with the prosperity of manufactures. Every nation, with a view to these great objects, ought to endeavor to possess within itself all the essentials of national supply. Alexander Hamilton, Report on Manufactures (1791), quoted in Drezner 2010: 3
[I]t is a very common device that when anyone has attained the summit of greatness, he kicks away the ladder by which he has climbed up, in order to deprive others of the means of climbing up after him. Friedrich List, The National System of Political Economy (1841), quoted in CWE p. 59

CWE pp. 57–59: Hamilton — industrialization “key for the ‘independence and security’ of the United States”; List — free trade would enable “the English manufactures to monopolise the markets of all nations.”

1 · Building power · List’s conditions

  • Productive power — derives “from mental and physical powers of the individuals; from their social, municipal, and political conditions and institutions; from the natural resources” p. 59
  • Moderate, not prohibitory — duties that “entirely cut off foreign competition, injure the country which imposes them … and indolence is fostered” p. 60
  • Temporary — “until that manufacturing power is strong enough no longer to have any reason to fear foreign competition” p. 60
  • Manufacturing only — free trade in raw materials and agricultural products; List backed repeal of the Corn Laws p. 60
  • Nationality — “the intermediate interest between those of individualism and of entire humanity” p. 61

Helleiner, CWE pp. 59–61, quoting List [1841] 1909. Same pages: protection for “temperate” countries only; a “Continental alliance against the British supremacy.”

2 · Using leverage · Farrell & Newman 2019

Asymmetric network structures create the potential for “weaponized interdependence,” in which some states are able to leverage interdependent relations to coerce others. Farrell & Newman 2019: 45

[T]hey respectively rely on the panopticon and chokepoint effects of networks. In the former, advantaged states use their network position to extract informational advantages vis-à-vis adversaries, whereas in the latter, they can cut adversaries off from network flows. Farrell & Newman 2019: 46

Farrell & Newman, “Weaponized Interdependence,” International Security 44(1), 2019, pp. 45–46 (Depth reading). Diagram: a hub-and-spoke network as described on p. 45 (“some nodes are ‘hubs,’ and are far more connected than others”); illustrative, not data.

2 · Using leverage · case: Russia’s banks and reserves, 2022–2025

Chokepoint, 2022

  • Mar 2, 2022: EU removes seven Russian banks from SWIFT (VTB, Otkritie, Novikombank, Promsvyazbank, Rossiya, Sovcombank, VEB); Sberbank and Gazprombank exempted
  • ~$300 bn of Central Bank of Russia reserves immobilised; ~€185 bn held at Euroclear (Belgium)

What happened to the money, 2024–2025

  • Jun 2024: G7 $50 bn ERA loan to Ukraine, serviced from windfall profits on the assets
  • Dec 12, 2025: EU prohibits transfers of the immobilised assets back to Russia
  • Dec 19, 2025: EU leaders drop the €90 bn “reparations loan” drawn on the assets after Belgium objects; fund a €90 bn loan by EU borrowing instead
For me, the reparations loan was not a good idea. We avoided stepping into a precedent that risks undermining legal certainty worldwide. Bart De Wever, Prime Minister of Belgium, Dec 19, 2025 (AP)

3 · Paying for security · Carr, Gilpin, Kirshner
